Solo-ship trader: fly your own ship, haul, trade, and chart drift POIs

- Start aboard your own starter skiff, persistent across sessions. Losing the ship wipes the run and offers a restart.
- Keep the reactor and engine alive: trim rods and coolant to stay in the nominal band, recover from scrams and blackouts, manage the shared power budget, tune and spool the engine, and watch wear.
- Plot hub-to-hub voyages from the nav console with live destination, ETA, and countdown. Cancel, flame out when dry, call for a paid tow, or hail a free rescue drone that arrives after about 75 seconds.
- Haul physical crates: pick up, carry (slower while loaded), rotate, set down, unpack into secured stores, and repack. Carried crates ride in your hands and drop where you see them.
- Trade six goods across New Anchorage and Kepler Yard: credit-priced buy rows, per-hub stock that restocks slowly, per-crate and sell-all-bay selling with live payouts, and food eaten per leg before starvation bites.
- Pack what you buy with the physics packing bench: drag goods into the crate, rotate pieces, seat the lid, then seal. Staged goods show running prices and the seal total.
- Chart the Drift: two hubs plus the Kestrel derelict and Vigil beacon, rumor hints for unsurveyed sites, multi-stop voyages with VIA detours, flyby surveys that fill the manifest, direct landings at POIs, and a draft throttle that trades burn time for fuel.
- Fuel from the engine bunker: load loose cells at the engine console while docked, compare HAVE vs NEED before plotting, and watch the gauge drain continuously in flight — flameout always means empty, and any restored fuel resumes the burn.
- Galaxy chart stays put in flight: smooth planet motion, a frozen departure route that shrinks toward arrival, and a burn chevron that flips to braking past the midpoint.
- Punchier sound: cabin carbine crack with hull conduction and brass, electromechanical cash register with bell, and layered metal impacts for bullets.
